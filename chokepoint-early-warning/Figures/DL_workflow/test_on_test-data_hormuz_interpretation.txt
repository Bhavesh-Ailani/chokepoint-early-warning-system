CV was far too optimistic. Random Forest’s MAE goes from 9.0 in CV to 27.2 on the test months. Months are correlated in time, so random CV folds share information. Put this gap in the report.
Every R² is negative. All the models do worse than simply predicting the test months’ own average. They still beat Constant on MAE by 13–24%, so they capture some of the signal.
There’s a level shift. From the Constant row, the test-period variance is about 615 (σ ≈ 25). The gap between the training average and the test average is about 31 points. The Hormuz months sit at a price level that never appears in training, so no model trained on those years can predict it well. Check the direction of that gap in the scatter plot.
NN_A beats Random Forest on test by only 1.5 points of MAE, and that comes from a single test period of 251 rows. NN_B and NN_C are both worse than Random Forest, and in CV Random Forest clearly won. So on “is the neural network worth it”: the gain is small, it doesn’t hold up across the three settings, and it isn’t backed by CV. The honest verdict is not convincingly.
This supports how the project is built. The classifier in WF1 (will a shock happen?) is the usable early-warning tool. Predicting the exact size of a price jump during an unprecedented crisis is not reliable.
